**Anthropic PBC**[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-8) is an American [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") (AI) company headquartered in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), California. It has developed a series of [large language models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model "Large language model") (LLMs) named [Claude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_\(language_model\) "Claude \(language model\)") and has a focus on [AI safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_safety "AI safety").[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-9) Anthropic was founded in 2021 by former members of [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI "OpenAI"), including siblings [Daniela Amodei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniela_Amodei "Daniela Amodei") and [Dario Amodei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dario_Amodei "Dario Amodei"), who are president and CEO, respectively.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-10) The company is [privately held](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privately_held_company "Privately held company") and as of May 2026[[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit) had an estimated value of $965 billion.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Babu-2026-11)
## History
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: 2021–2023")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Dario_Amodei_at_TechCrunch_Disrupt_2023_01.jpg/250px-Dario_Amodei_at_TechCrunch_Disrupt_2023_01.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dario_Amodei_at_TechCrunch_Disrupt_2023_01.jpg)[Dario Amodei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dario_Amodei "Dario Amodei"), co-founder and CEO
Anthropic was founded in January 2021[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-incorporation-1) by seven former employees of OpenAI, including siblings Daniela Amodei and Dario Amodei, the latter of whom was OpenAI's Vice President of Research.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Henshall-2023-12)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-VentureBeat-2023-13) They raised $124 million in May 2021.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-14) In the summer of 2022, Anthropic finished [training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning "Machine learning") the first version of [Claude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_\(language_model\) "Claude \(language model\)") but did not immediately release it, citing a need for further internal safety testing and a desire to avoid initiating a potentially hazardous race to develop increasingly powerful AI systems.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-15) It was released in March 2023.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-16)
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: 2024–present")]
In 2024, Anthropic hired several notable employees from OpenAI, including [Jan Leike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Leike "Jan Leike") and [John Schulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Schulman "John Schulman").[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-17) In May 2025, the company announced Claude 4. It also introduced new API capabilities, including the [Model Context Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_Context_Protocol "Model Context Protocol") (MCP) connector.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-claude_4_a-18) The company hosted its inaugural developer conference that month.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-19) Also in May, Anthropic launched a [web search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_search "Web search") API that enables Claude to access real-time information from the internet.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-20) Claude Code, Anthropic's coding assistant, transitioned from research preview to general availability.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-claude_4_a-18)
In September 2025, Anthropic announced that it would stop selling its products to groups majority-owned by Chinese, Russian, Iranian, or North Korean entities due to national security concerns.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-21) In October 2025, Anthropic announced a cloud partnership with Google, giving it access to up to one million of Google's custom [Tensor Processing Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_Processing_Unit "Tensor Processing Unit") (TPUs), potentially adding more than one gigawatt of AI compute capacity by 2026.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-22) In November, [Nvidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia "Nvidia") and [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft") were expected to invest up to $15 billion in Anthropic, and Anthropic said it would buy $30 billion of computing capacity from [Microsoft Azure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Azure "Microsoft Azure") running on Nvidia AI systems.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-23)
In November 2025, Anthropic said that hackers sponsored by the [Chinese government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_government "Chinese government") used Claude to perform automated cyberattacks against around 30 global organizations. The hackers bypassed Anthropic's safeguards by pretending it was for defensive testing.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-24)[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-25) In December 2025, Anthropic acquired [Bun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bun_\(software\) "Bun \(software\)") to improve Claude Code's speed and stability.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-26) The same month, Anthropic signed a multi-year, $200 million partnership with [Snowflake Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflake_Inc. "Snowflake Inc.") to make Claude models available through that company's platform.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-27)
In February 2026, Anthropic aired two commercials during [Super Bowl LX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LX "Super Bowl LX")[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-28)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Kiefer-2026-29) as part of a broader marketing campaign called "A Time and a Place", with four ads created by [Mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_\(advertising_agency\) "Mother \(advertising agency\)"). Each ad depicts AI assistants suddenly pivoting to promoting a fictional product in the middle of a conversation. Anthropic said that Claude will stay ad-free, in contrast to its competitor OpenAI, which introduced ads to the free version of ChatGPT.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Kiefer-2026-29) Also in February 2026, Anthropic donated $20 million to the nonprofit organization [Public First Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_First_Action "Public First Action"), which has announced plans to support political candidates that support regulation of AI.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-30)
In 2026, Anthropic refused the [United States Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense")'s (DoD) demand to remove contractual restrictions prohibiting the use of its AI technology for domestic [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance "Surveillance") and [fully autonomous weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_robot "Military robot"). After the refusal, the DoD designated the company a "supply chain risk" and barred all U.S. military private contractors, suppliers, and partners from doing business with the firm (see [Anthropic–United States Department of Defense dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic%E2%80%93United_States_Department_of_Defense_dispute "Anthropic–United States Department of Defense dispute")).[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-31)[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-32)

## Product
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Product")]
Main article: [Claude (language model)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_\(language_model\) "Claude \(language model\)")
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Claude_AI_symbol.svg/250px-Claude_AI_symbol.svg.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Claude_AI_symbol.svg)Logo of Claude
Anthropic's flagship product line is the "Claude" series of large language models,[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-50) which some employees consider a reference to mathematician [Claude Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Shannon "Claude Shannon").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Roose-5) One of the techniques used to [fine-tune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tune "Fine-tune") Claude models is called "constitutional AI", in which the LLM is trained to adhere to a set of principles called a constitution.[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-51) The company makes the models available via a web interface,[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Anthropic-2023-52) an API,[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Anthropic-2023-52) [Amazon Bedrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Bedrock "Amazon Bedrock"),[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-53) an iOS app,[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-54) and Mac and Windows desktop apps.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-55)
Claude Code is a [command-line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command-line_interface "Command-line interface") [AI agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_agent "AI agent") designed for coding. "Cowork" is an equivalent with a [graphical user interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphical_user_interface "Graphical user interface"), intended to be simpler to use.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-56) While Claude Code is tailored toward [developers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_developer "Software developer"), Cowork is designed for non-technical users, enabling them to automate file and [task management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_management "Task management") (including reading, editing and writing new files) without requiring [programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programming "Computer programming") knowledge.[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-57)
With the advent of Claude Code, [vibe coding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibe_coding "Vibe coding"), a programming approach in which users describe desired outcomes in [natural language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language "Natural language") and let an [AI agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_agent "AI agent") write the code, became increasingly popular. The term was coined by AI researcher [Andrej Karpathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrej_Karpathy "Andrej Karpathy") in February 2025 and rapidly gained popularity as AI coding tools became more ubiquitous.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-58)
### Release history
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Release history")]
Claude's first two versions, Claude and Claude Instant, were released in March 2023,[[59]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-59)[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-60) but only Anthropic-approved users could use them.[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-vox-61) The next iteration, Claude 2, was launched to the public in July 2023.[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Milmo-2023-62) In March 2024, Anthropic released three language models: Claude 3 Opus, Claude 3 Sonnet, and Claude 3 Haiku, in decreasing order of performance.[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Nu%C3%B1ez_2024-03-04-63) In June 2024, it released Claude 3.5 Sonnet.[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-64) In May 2025, Anthropic released Claude 4 Opus and Sonnet.[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-65) In February 2026, it released Claude Opus 4.6,[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-66) followed by Sonnet 4.6.[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-67) In April 2026, Anthropic announced [Mythos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Mythos "Claude Mythos").[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-68)

### U.S. military and intelligence
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: U.S. military and intelligence")]
Main article: [Anthropic–United States Department of Defense dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic%E2%80%93United_States_Department_of_Defense_dispute "Anthropic–United States Department of Defense dispute")
In November 2024, Anthropic partnered with [Palantir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palantir_Technologies "Palantir Technologies") and [Amazon Web Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Web_Services "Amazon Web Services") to provide the Claude model to U.S. intelligence and defense agencies.[[83]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-83)[[84]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-84) In June 2025, Anthropic announced a "Claude Gov" model. _[Ars Technica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars_Technica "Ars Technica")_ reported that as of June 2025, it was in use at multiple U.S. national security agencies.[[85]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-85) As of February 2026, Anthropic's partnership with Palantir makes Claude the only AI model used in classified missions.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-FT1-86) In July 2025, the [United States Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense") (DoD) announced that Anthropic had received a $200 million contract for AI in the military, along with Google, OpenAI, and [xAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceXAI "SpaceXAI").[[87]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-87)
According to _[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")_ , the U.S. military used Claude in its [2026 raid on Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_intervention_in_Venezuela "2026 United States intervention in Venezuela") to capture President [Nicolás Maduro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Maduro "Nicolás Maduro"). Exactly how Claude was used is unknown. The intervention resulted in the deaths of 83 people.[[88]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-88)[[89]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-89) Anthropic's usage policy prohibits directly using Claude for domestic surveillance or in fully autonomous weapons.[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-NBC-News-2026-90) White House officials said that these restrictions prevented federal contractors working with the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI "FBI") and [Secret Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secret_Service "United States Secret Service") from using it.[[91]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-91) Tension regarding this policy has arisen with [The Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon "The Pentagon") and the Trump administration.[[90]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-NBC-News-2026-90)[[92]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-92)
On February 24, 2026, Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Hegseth "Pete Hegseth") threatened to cut Anthropic out of the DoD's supply chain by February 27 if Anthropic did not permit unrestricted use of Claude, or he would invoke the [Defense Production Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Production_Act_of_1950 "Defense Production Act of 1950") to assert unrestricted use without an agreement.[[86]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-FT1-86)[[93]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-93) On February 26, Anthropic rejected the Pentagon's demands to drop AI safeguards.[[94]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-94) On February 27, after the deadline, President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") ordered U.S. government agencies to stop using models developed by Anthropic.[[95]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-95) The action was described as retaliatory.[[96]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-96)[[97]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-97)
Two days later, it was reported that the US military had used Claude via Palantir's [Maven Smart System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Maven "Project Maven") during its attacks on Iran at the outset of the [2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war "2026 Iran war").[[98]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-98)[[99]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-99) It was later announced that the Pentagon was investigating whether Claude played a role in the [U.S. strike on the Iranian girls' school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Minab_school_attack "2026 Minab school attack") that killed over 170 people, mostly children.[[100]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-100) On March 5, the Pentagon officially deemed Anthropic a supply chain risk, after which the company said it saw "no choice but to challenge ... in court".[[101]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-101) In March, leaders from Google, Amazon, Apple and Microsoft supported Anthropic's lawsuit against the DoD.[[102]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-102) On March 11, a Pentagon memo revealed its decision that Anthropic's AI tools may be used in rare circumstances beyond the previously announced six-month phase-out period if deemed necessary for national security.[[103]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-103) On March 26, the federal judge presiding over the case issued a temporary injunction against the Pentagon's actions, writing, "This appears to be classic First Amendment retaliation."[[104]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-104)[[105]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-105)

### Project Panama
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Project Panama")]
In January 2026, unsealed court filings from a [2024 class-action copyright lawsuit against Anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#Legal_issues) revealed the existence of the company's confidential "Project Panama" operation. In an internal planning document, Project Panama is described as Anthropic's "effort to [destructively scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_scanning#Destructive_scanning_methods "Book scanning") all the books in the world".[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-washpost_projectpanama_jan2026-108) To this end, the company purchased millions of used books from online retailers such as [Better World Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_World_Books "Better World Books"), sliced off their spines and scanned their pages in order to [train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model#Training "Large language model") Claude.[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-washpost_projectpanama_jan2026-108)[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Edwards-2025-109) The paper was then recycled. Tom Turvey, who helped create [Google Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Books "Google Books"), was hired for the operation. According to the Project Panama planning document, Anthropic did not "want it to be known that [it was] working on this".[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-washpost_projectpanama_jan2026-108) Judge [William Alsup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Alsup "William Alsup") ruled that the destruction of legally purchased books constituted [fair use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_use "Fair use"), in contrast to Anthropic's prior use of pirated copies.[[108]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-washpost_projectpanama_jan2026-108)[[109]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Edwards-2025-109)
## Research
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Research")]
### Constitutional AI
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Constitutional AI")]
Main article: [Claude (language model) § Constitutional AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_\(language_model\)#Constitutional_AI "Claude \(language model\)")
According to Anthropic, Constitutional AI (CAI) is a framework developed to align AI systems with human values and ensure that they are helpful, harmless, and honest.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Henshall-2023-12)[[110]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Bai-2022-110) Within this framework, a set of guidelines known as the "constitution" describes the desired behavior of the AI system. Claude 2's constitution includes phrases derived from documents such as the [1948 Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights "Universal Declaration of Human Rights") and Apple's terms of service. For example, one rule from the UN Declaration applied in Claude 2's constitution is "Please choose the response that most supports and encourages freedom, equality and a sense of brotherhood."[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Milmo-2023-62) The AI system evaluates the generated output and then adjusts the AI models to better fit the constitution.[[110]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Bai-2022-110)
### Interpretability
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Interpretability")]
Anthropic carries out and publishes research on the [interpretability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpretability_\(machine_learning\) "Interpretability \(machine learning\)") of [machine learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning "Machine learning") systems.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Henshall-2023-12)[[111]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-111) It has done research on "features" (patterns of neural activation in a [neural network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_network_\(machine_learning\) "Neural network \(machine learning\)") that correspond to concepts). In 2024, using a compute-intensive technique called "[dictionary learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictionary_learning "Dictionary learning")", Anthropic identified millions of these patterns in Claude, such as one associated with the [Golden Gate Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Gate_Bridge "Golden Gate Bridge").[[112]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-112)[[113]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-113)[[114]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-114) In March 2025, Anthropic's research suggested that multilingual LLMs partially process information in a conceptual space before converting it to the appropriate language. It also found evidence that LLMs can sometimes plan ahead. For example, when writing poetry, Claude identifies potential rhyming words before generating a line that ends with one of these words.[[115]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-115)[[116]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-116)
### Automation
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Automation")]
In September 2025, Anthropic released a report saying that businesses primarily use AI for automation rather than collaboration, with three-quarters of companies that work with Claude using it for "full task delegation".[[117]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-117) Earlier in the year, CEO Dario Amodei predicted that AI would wipe out white-collar jobs, especially entry-level jobs in finance, law, and consulting.[[118]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-118)[[119]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-119)
## Legal issues
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anthropic&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Legal issues")]
On October 18, 2023, Anthropic was sued by [Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord_Music_Group "Concord Music Group"), [Universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Music_Group "Universal Music Group"), [ABKCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABKCO_Records "ABKCO Records"), and other music publishers for, per the complaint, "systematic and widespread infringement of their copyrighted song lyrics."[[120]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-120)[[121]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-121)[[122]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-122) They alleged that the company used copyrighted material without permission in the form of song lyrics.[[123]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Masse-2023-123) The plaintiffs asked for up to $150,000 for each work infringed upon by Anthropic, citing infringement of copyright laws.[[123]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Masse-2023-123) In the lawsuit, the plaintiffs support their allegations of copyright violations by citing several examples of Anthropic's Claude model outputting copied lyrics from songs such as [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry "Katy Perry")'s "[Roar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roar_\(song\) "Roar \(song\)")" and [Gloria Gaynor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Gaynor "Gloria Gaynor")'s "[I Will Survive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Will_Survive "I Will Survive")".[[123]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Masse-2023-123) Additionally, the plaintiffs alleged that even given some prompts that did not directly state a song name, the model responded with modified lyrics based on original work.[[123]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-Masse-2023-123) On January 16, 2024, Anthropic claimed that the music publishers were not unreasonably harmed and that the examples noted by the plaintiffs were merely bugs.[[124]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-124)
[Kirk Wallace Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirk_W._Johnson "Kirk W. Johnson"), [Andrea Bartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Bartz "Andrea Bartz"), and [Charles Graeber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Graeber "Charles Graeber").[[125]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-125) On June 23, 2025, the [United States District Court for the Northern District of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Northern_District_of_California "United States District Court for the Northern District of California") granted summary judgment for Anthropic that the use of digital copies of the plaintiffs' works (inter alia) for the purpose of training Anthropic's LLMs was a [fair use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_use "Fair use"). But it found that Anthropic had used millions of pirated library copies, and that such use could not constitute fair use. Therefore, the case was ordered to go to trial on the pirated copies used to create Anthropic's central library and the resulting damages.[[126]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-126) In September 2025, Anthropic agreed to pay authors $1.5 billion to settle the case, amounting to $3,000 per book plus interest. The proposed settlement, pending judge's approval, stands as the largest copyright resolution in U.S. history.[[127]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-127)[[128]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-128)
In June 2025, [Reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit "Reddit") sued Anthropic for "unlawful and unfair business acts", alleging that Anthropic was in violation of Reddit's user agreement by training its models on users' personal data without their consent.[[129]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-129)[[130]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic#cite_note-130)
